In this article, we will attempt to explain why the Ukrainian military’s use of drones in combat missions is just as reprehensible as the Russian military’s use of drones. We’ll begin with a quote from a Czech daily newspaper:

The war in Ukraine has escalated in recent days into a battle over energy infrastructure. Russia is systematically destroying Ukrainian gas facilities in the Kharkiv region, while Ukrainian drones are striking targets deep inside Russian territory. Nighttime airstrikes on the outskirts of Moscow left ten people injured, including three Chinese citizens. (…) In Podolsk, drones struck civilian buildings and a highway, injuring two more women. An 11-year-old girl also had to seek medical attention due to a severe stress reaction. – source: https://ekonomickydenik.cz/ukrajinske-drony-ruske-vnitrozemi-zraneni-cizinci/

What political and strategic conclusions do supporters of the “Ukrainian people’s resistance” draw from this? We see no critical self-reflection here. Their perspective remains as tragic as ever. First, according to them, Russia is the sole aggressor in this war. Second, they continue to call for “more military equipment for the Ukrainian army,” which they claim is solely defending the Ukrainian civilian population. Third, they continue to raise funds for the manufacturers and pilots of Ukrainian drones, which they describe as instruments of liberation and means of defense.

Attacks by the Ukrainian military using drones have repeatedly resulted in injuries, deaths, and trauma among civilians in Russia. Pro-Ukrainian media, including those labeled as anarchist, however, unanimously declare: “Ukraine has been defending itself against Russian aggression for five years.” Thus, it is considered aggression when the Russian military bombs infrastructure in Kharkiv, but when the Ukrainian military bombs similar infrastructure in Russia, it is an act of self-defense.(1)

We would search in vain for any substantial difference between what pro-Ukrainian fighters and their supporters consider to be defense and what reasonable people generally regard as a ruthless attack on innocent people. It is clear that both sides—Russia and Ukraine—are the same in this regard. Each side accuses the other of spreading terror, while describing the terror it itself inflicts as necessary self-defense.

That is simply the essence of war propaganda and practice. The ruling classes and their minions on all sides spread destruction, but it is the working class—which has no interest in waging war—that suffers the most from its consequences. This is summed up very well by a comment that was recently posted in a discussion thread under a podcast on the ANews website. In this podcast, there was a discussion about a controversial interview in which Margaret Killjoy asks questions of a person named Kafa, who operates drones in Ukrainian military operations and describes this as the only correct anarchist strategy during the war. Currently I have family in two cities that are being attacked by ukrainian drones. I hear about my cousin being scared to go to school and my grandma describing the sound of drones while she gives me updates about her chronic heart condition. My mom has been having panic attacks for years now because the reality that our family might get killed before we get to see them again is very real. I mostly don’t talk about this with my friends. Most people don’t have anything to say to me when I bring it up. I know that this is much less intense than what people in ukraine are experiencing. I do not see myself as capable of speaking on what its like to live under an active military occupation, in an active War zone.
