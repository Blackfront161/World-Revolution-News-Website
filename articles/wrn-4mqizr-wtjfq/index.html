Wollongong Against War and Nukes has joined with many others in this city to show our opposition towards plans to turn our home into a military town. This includes a rally of several thousand people down the main street of Port Kembla in 2023.

As well as more than 40 local organisations, including health and environment bodies, trade unions and church groups, who have signed the Port Kembla Declaration, demanding the federal government rule out a submarine base in our region.

Port Kembla residents have widely opposed having a base in the area and thousands in our community have come together to declare that this is “No Place for a Nuclear Base”.

We are concerned that the presence of nuclear submarines would make the Illawarra a high priority target for conventional or nuclear attack. We are concerned that US submarines carrying nuclear weapons could dock in Port Kembla. We are concerned about the risk of nuclear accidents. We are concerned about becoming a site for the storage of nuclear waste. We are concerned about the social, economic and environmental impact of a nuclear submarine base in Port Kembla. We are concerned that, even in a best-case scenario, a nuclear submarine base would have a highly detrimental impact on our community.

We love Port Kembla and the Illawarra region; we don’t want to lose the things we value about this place.

We have been told that the decision on the location of a nuclear submarine base has been deferred. This is no comfort — if anything it increases our fears that decisions are being made in secret, without community consultation and overriding local dissent.

I take a lot of inspiration from the deep desire for peace that people in our city have shown over many generations. This opposition to war includes the famous “Pig Iron Bob” Dalfram Dispute and, in more recent times, protests against the invasions of Vietnam, Afghanistan and Iraq.

We have repeatedly said “No to war!” For many decades, the local community has also said “No to nuclear war!”

Wollongong was first declared a nuclear-free city in 1980. In 2019, Wollongong City Council signed up to the International Campaign for the Abolition of Nuclear Weapons Cities Appeal. In 2022 and 2025, Wollongong City Council again reaffirmed the city’s nuclear-free status.

Visits or the stationing of nuclear submarines in Port Kembla is seriously dangerous.

Nuclear reactors pose major risks for people and the environment. Unlike civilian reactors, most information about military naval reactors is kept classified. Nuclear-powered submarines have a long history of accidents, many of which are again shrouded in secrecy.

Seven hundred and eighty nine nuclear safety events have been recorded at Scotland’s naval bases since 2006 and the British Ministry of Defence has acknowledged that many incidents had “actual or high potential for radioactive release to the environment”. But it has have refused to say what actually happened in any of the incidents, or when they occurred — raising concerns about whether local residents would be informed if similar incidents occurred in Port Kembla.

Even if the probability of an accident is “low”, the level of consequence of a nuclear submarine accident is extremely high, given that the submarine’s nuclear reactors are fuelled by weapons-grade highly enriched uranium.

The release of secret documents, obtained under FOI legislation in May, demonstrates that planning is being hidden from the public and the risks are being hidden from the communities affected.

Secret NSW government planning documents have considered what should happen in case of an accident or explosion on one of the submarines. Australia’s nuclear safety agency assessed that an accident at a submarine base here would require evacuating most residential streets in Port Kembla. Port Kembla school — the school my two kids attend — would be in the immediate evacuation zone.

Our community has never been properly informed of or consulted about this risk. It is unclear how large the real evacuation zone would be if an accident or an attack on the base occurred. Or for how long the port, or the city, its land, water and air could be contaminated.

Our community knows all about living with a legacy of toxic waste. I have had my garden soil tested for heavy metals and my attic cleaned because of the accumulation of toxic dust over decades.
