The chief of the Okanagan Indian Band says about 230 homes have been destroyed by a raging wildfire near Vernon, B.C., in one of the worst wildfire losses in recent Canadian history.

Dan Wilson said 30 homes of band members and 200 non-band homes had burned in the Bradley Creek fire, which evacuees said was fanned by powerful winds over the weekend.

Wilson said during a news conference on Tuesday that the reserve “looked like a war zone” when he took a helicopter tour of the area, at the northern end of Okanagan Lake in the B.C. Interior.

“It was hard to recognize my community from the air with all the structures and the forest gone.”

The wildfire has been one of the most devastating events in the band’s history, he said.

Wilson described the event as “surreal,” saying the scope of destruction was just sinking in.

The fire started late Friday and was fanned by winds gusting up to 115 kilometres per hour.

“The point that I really want to make to everyone is that the fire spread so fast that some of our members only had five minutes to evacuate,” Wilson said.

Wilson said his flyover showed just how close the nation came to losing key infrastructure, like the powwow arbour, a daycare centre and the new school, all of which were saved by aircraft saturating the area with water.

“But that was crossing my mind because we’re so proud of our Cultural Immersion School. We just opened it up and it’s not only our band members that go to that school. It’s the local residents at Killiney and West Shores and the surrounding areas.”

Reserve resident Louis Fred and his wife Shorty said they barely made it out on Saturday, with the wind-driven flames on their heels.

“I’ve been living here all my life, and I’ve really never seen anything like this one here, this weekend here,” Louis Fred said. “Even 2021 was bad. It was huge. But the wind wasn’t nothing like (that) compared to this one.”

A 2024 wildfire in Jasper, Alta., razed about 350 homes, while the 2023 McDougall Creek wildfire destroyed about 200 homes, also around Okanagan Lake.

The Freds are among about 8,000 people who have been forced from their homes across B.C., according to Emergency Management Minister Kelly Greene.

She told a separate briefing on Tuesday that there are 37 evacuation orders across the province, although she added that orders and alerts were changing hourly.

The orders triggered by the Bradley Creek fire expanded on Tuesday, as the Regional District of North Okanagan added more than 40 properties as the fire blocked some road escape routes.

The EmergencyBC system said on social media that residents affected by the expanded order will be escorted out in a convoy.

The BC Wildfire Service said there were about 120 active wildfires across the province, with roughly 50 of them burning out-of-control.

In addition to Bradley Creek, major fires are burning in the Fraser Canyon, Thompson-Nicola and East Kootenay regions near communities, including Boston Bar, Clinton and Kimberley.

The wildfire service said Tuesday that fire activity is expected to increase this week “as hot and dry conditions return for the majority” of B.C., although winds are forecasted to remain light.

When asked why the government hasn’t yet declared a state of emergency, Greene said it was unnecessary, as every jurisdiction that was fighting a wildfire already had a local state of emergency, enabling firefighters to take the action needed to protect communities and lives.

The designation would allow the government to restrict travel in the province or ration supplies, such as fuel or accommodations, but Greene said those options weren’t immediately needed.

Jeff Dunn, the fire centre manager in the Kamloops region, said winds fanned the Bradley Creek fire to reach 26 square kilometres very quickly, but it had not grown much since the cold front moved through.

He said they were quickly trying to get control lines around the fire in order try to hold it when the weather shifts again over the next few days.

“Kind of bringing in additional resources to make sure that we have everything locked in place and are able to control any additional spread.”

When the fire bore down on the Freds home on the Okanagan Indian Band reserve on Saturday, the couple and friends and family members initially tried to fight the flames.
